# Language and Naming

## Overview

The [Founders](../species/Founders.md) imposed one administrative language across the whole [Solar System](../solar-system/Solar%20System.md), the way any empire running quotas, shipping manifests, and a rail network has to. Everyone spoke it because everyone worked for them. It is still called **the Standard**.

The Founders died in the [Virus War](../species/Founders.md#virus-war) and took the thing holding the Standard together with them. Three thousand years, a dark age, and a severed teleporter network later, every region speaks a drifted version of the same language — mutually intelligible, obviously related, and full of local giveaways. The relationship is US/UK/Australian English, not English/German. A [Vall](../moko/dorath/vall.md) dwarf and a [Vael](../moko/aethos/vael.md) priest understand each other fine and can each tell within a sentence where the other is from.

This document is the standard pattern for coining names. The short version: **there are two layers, and which layer a name comes from tells you when it was named and by whom.**

## The Two Layers

Every name in the world belongs to one of two strata. This is the single rule that makes names stop feeling arbitrary.

### Layer 1 — Founder Standard (the high register)

Latinate. Used for anything the Founders themselves named or administered: worlds, continents, their stations and installations, technical vocabulary, institutions that inherited their authority, and the personal names of the classes that served them closest.

Existing examples: **Aethos**, **Dorath**, **Vael**, **Raxmar**, **Moko**, **Petra**, **Dranus Dovo**, **Umbro Augusto**, **Isaera**, **Aurora**, **Elliptism**.

### Layer 2 — Vernacular (the low register)

Transparent compounds of plain, everyday words. Used for everything people named for themselves *after* the Founders were gone: wildlife, plants, tools, trades, and settlements founded in the [Age of Darkness](../ages/Age%20%283%29%20of%20Darkness.md) or later.

Existing examples: **Hexguard**, **Flergburg**, **Mysthaven**, **Fangwurms**, **Glowgrass**, **Trapjaws**, **Blade Grass**, **Tombstone Flower**, **Jet Shells**, **Claw Flowers**, **Sky Jellies**.

### Why this works

It maps onto how English actually splits its registers — Latin and French for law, administration, and science; Germanic for the things you touch every day. A *sovereign* rules but a *king* is your neighbor's word for him. It gives you a built-in, free signal: a Latinate name means old and official, a compound name means recent and lived-in.

And it is already true in the files. **Vael** is "the oldest and largest city in all of Aethos," and it has a Founder name. **Hexguard** was built by [Dranus Dovo](../characters/dranus_dovo.md) inside living memory, and it has a compound name. The corpus was following this rule before it was written down.

## The Founder Naming Instinct

The Founders named things like a logistics company, because that is what they were. Their names are **assay results and job labels**, never poetry. This is the most distinctive thing about the world's naming and it should be protected.

Look at what they called the worlds — every one is a survey verdict on what could be extracted:

| Name | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Moko** | *yield* — the world that produces magic |
| **Null** | *none* — the world that produces no magic of its own |
| **Cinder** | *burnt* — scorched, marginal |
| **Fae** | *irregular* — magic that wouldn't classify |
| **Void** | *emptied* — a continent after they were done with it |

And what they called their processes: the **Quota**, **Extraction**, **Reassignment**, the **processing station**, **Magic Metal**, **Null Metal**. Flat, clinical, and every one of them a euphemism for feeding people to [Fangwurms](../biomes/eurytopic/fauna/Fangwurms.md).

**Rule: Founder-era coinages are blunt functional labels or bureaucratic euphemisms. Never ornamental, never sinister-sounding on purpose.** The horror is that the paperwork is polite.

## Founder Standard — Phonology

Use this inventory and names will sound like each other. Deviating from it is how a name ends up feeling random.

**Consonants in:** p b t d k g, f v, s z, m n, l r, w y, **th** /θ/, **x** /ks/

**Consonants out:** **sh**, **ch**, **j** /dʒ/, **ng**. These four sounds do not exist in Founder Standard. Excluding them is what makes the name-set cohere. (Check the corpus — there isn't one of them in the entire Latinate layer.)

**Vowels:** a e i o u, plus two signature digraphs:
- **ae** — the strongest marker in the language. Already in **Aethos**, **Vael**, **Is*ae*ra**, **Fae**.
- **au** — **Augusto**, **Aurora**.

Use **ae** freely when coining. It does more work than anything else to make a new name read as belonging here.

**Syllables:** (C)(r/l)V(C). Onset clusters take only *r* or *l* as the second element — **dr-, tr-, br-, gr-, pr-, fl-, kl-**. See *Dranus*, *Petra*, *Umbro*, *Flerg*. No **st-, sp-, sk-** at the start of a word in this layer.

**Stress:** penultimate, Latin-style. *DO-vo, PE-tra, au-GUS-to, i-SAE-ra, DRA-nus, AE-thos, DO-rath, RAX-mar, MO-ko.*

## Founder Standard — Roots and Suffixes

Names should *mean* something. This is the second cure for randomness: a coined name that decomposes into a root and a job-suffix will feel designed even to a reader who never learns the key.

### Suffixes

| Suffix | Sense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-os, -ath, -oth** | designated domain, zone (land scale) | Aeth**os**, Dor**ath** |
| **-mar** | station, terminal, post (site scale) | Rax**mar** |
| **-ael** | administrative seat | V**ael** |
| **-ex, -ax** | device, instrument | *Secax* |
| **-um, -ium** | substance, material | *Ferrium* |
| **-or** | agent, one who does | *Curor* |
| **-us** | personal name, masculine | Dran**us** |
| **-a, -aera** | personal name, feminine (*-aera* elevated) | Petr**a**, Isa**era** |
| **-o** | lineage / house name | Dov**o**, August**o**, Umbr**o** |

### Roots

Reverse-engineered from existing names, so the canon stays intact:

| Root | Sense | Yields |
|---|---|---|
| **aeth-** | high, upper, overseeing | **Aethos** — the overseeing domain. Fits: administered from Vael by [High Elves](../species/ancestries/High%20Elves.md). |
| **dor-** | gate, passage, throughput | **Dorath** — the throughput domain. Fits: the continent where raw material was processed. |
| **vael** | seat, hall | **Vael** — the Seat. The last Founder administrative capital still using its name. |
| **rax-** | rack, load, freight | **Raxmar** — the loading terminal. Fits: a port. |
| **mok-** | yield, output | **Moko** |
| **umbr-** | shade, shadow | **Umbro** — apt for [a man who ambushes from underwater](../characters/umbro_augusto.md). |
| **petr-** | stone | **Petra** |
| **august-** | increase, augment | **Augusto** |
| **dran-** | draw off, siphon | **Dranus** — apt, and he'd hate that you noticed. |
| **dov-** | consign, hand over | **Dovo** — a Vael haulage-and-delivery house. |
| **is-** + **aer-** | constant + breath, spirit | **Isaera** — "unwavering spirit." Fits a zealot. |

Spare roots for coining: **ferr-** iron · **sang-** blood · **lum-** light · **ten-** hold, keep · **sec-** cut · **greg-** gather · **cur-** run, course · **fix-** anchored · **pond-** weight · **nex-** link · **cav-** hollow · **sol-** alone

**To coin: pick a root for what it does, add the suffix for what scale it is.** A Founder repository becomes *Tenael*. A forge station becomes *Ferramar*. A severing tool becomes *Secax*. A buried conduit line becomes *Nexath*.

## Personal Names

| Pattern | Who uses it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Given + lineage** (*-us/-a* + *-o*) | Aethos administrative and merchant class — the families that served the Founders directly | Dranus Dovo, Umbro Augusto |
| **Single Latinate name** | Common Aethos folk | Petra |
| **Single name, no house** | High Elves claiming pure descent — attaching a house-name would imply you need to specify *which* line, and their whole claim is that there is only one | [Isaera](../characters/isaera.md) |
| **Native-pattern name** | Mysthaven and other regions with a strong local layer | Talwyn |

*Umbro* is a lineage name used as a given name — a child named for the house. Ordinary practice, and worth remembering that his was a [Flergburg](../moko/aethos/flergburg.md) druidic family with an Aethos house-name.

## Vernacular Compounding

The low register has exactly one rule: **Descriptor + Function, both words plain and transparent, no apostrophes, no invented roots, no hyphens.**

- Settlements: *Hex* + *guard*, *Flerg* + *burg*, *Myst* + *haven*
- Wildlife: *Glow* + *grass*, *Fang* + *wurm*, *Trap* + *jaw*, *Blade* + *Grass*, *Jet* + *Shells*

Anyone hearing the name should be able to guess what the thing is. That's the point — these names were coined by farmers and sailors, not administrators.

**Coining new ones:** *Coldwatch*, *Kilnbridge*, *Saltgate*, *Ashfen*, *Rimehold* for settlements; *Sawmoss*, *Drownvine*, *Spitterfly* for wildlife.

**Spelling convention:** close the compound when both halves are one syllable (*Glowgrass*, *Trapjaw*, *Fangwurm*); leave it open when either half is longer (*Tombstone Flower*, *Vampire Vine*). The corpus is currently inconsistent on this — *Blade Grass* and *Claw Flowers* would close to *Bladegrass* and *Clawflowers* under the rule, if you want to normalize.

The **-burg** / **-wurm** spellings with the archaic *u* are a [Dorath](../moko/dorath/dorath.md) convention that spread the way British *-our* and *-re* spellings did. Aethos writes *Flergburg* because that's how the word arrived.

## Dialects

One language, five accents on [Moko](../moko/Moko.md), plus the off-world branches. These are sound shifts and spelling habits, not different vocabularies.

| Feature | **Vaelish** (Vael, Church) | **Plainspeech** (Hexguard, plains) | **Vallic** (Vall) | **Mistspeech** (Mysthaven) | **Dock Cant** (Raxmar) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *ae* | /aɪ/, full diphthong | /eɪ/ | /a/, flattened | /aɪə/, lengthened | /eɪ/ |
| *th* /θ/ | preserved | preserved | → /t/ | → /ð/ | → /f/ |
| *v* | /v/ | /v/ | → /f/ | /v/ | /v/ |
| final *-us* | preserved in full | clipped to *-s* or dropped | preserved, stressed hard | softened to *-uh* | dropped |
| *r* | tapped | retroflex | rolled | dropped after vowels | rolled |
| stress | penultimate | pulled to first syllable | first syllable, heavy | penultimate, lengthened | first syllable |

**Worked example — the name *Dranus*:**
Vaelish *DRAH-nus* · Plainspeech *Drans* / *Dran* · Vallic *TRAN-nus* · Mistspeech *Draa-nuh* · Dock Cant *Dran*

**Worked example — *Aethos*:**
Vaelish /ˈaɪθos/ · Plainspeech /ˈeɪθəs/ · Vallic /ˈatos/ · Mistspeech /ˈaɪðoʊ/ · Cant /ˈeɪfəs/

### Vaelish — the prestige dialect

Conservative and self-consciously correct. Full Latinate endings, Founder loanwords preferred for anything technical, long formal clauses. The Church of the Founders treats the Standard as liturgy, so Vaelish changes slowest of the Aethos dialects — by policy, not by accident. Its speakers regard everyone else's speech as decay. *(Analogue: ecclesiastical Latin / RP.)*

### Plainspeech — the leveler

The innovating majority dialect, spoken across the Aethos farms and dominant in [Hexguard](../moko/aethos/hexguard.md). Clips endings, drops case-like distinctions, and swaps Latinate terms for compounds the moment a compound will do — an *artifex* became an *artificer* and is on its way to being a *tinker*. Most new coinages in the world enter through Plainspeech. *(Analogue: General American.)*

### Vallic — the hardener

Consonant-heavy and front-stressed. Hardens *v* to *f* and *th* to *t*, rolls its *r*, keeps the archaic *-burg* and *-wurm* spellings. [Dwarf](../species/ancestries/Dwarves.md) and [Gnome](../species/ancestries/Gnomes.md) guild trades built enormous transparent compounds for technical work, so Vallic has precise vernacular words for metalwork and machinery where other dialects fall back on Founder loans. A Vallic speaker says *Dorat*, and says *Fall* for their own country. *(Analogue: Scots with Norse loading.)*

### Mistspeech — the preserver

Lenition and length. *d* softens toward /ð/, final consonants drop, vowels stretch. [Blood Elves](../species/ancestries/Blood%20Elves.md) were isolated in [Mysthaven](../moko/dorath/mysthaven.md) and long-lived, so their dialect preserves *older* Standard vocabulary than Vaelish does — including the terms the Church has quietly replaced with euphemisms. They have never stopped using the plain old word for what Magic Metal is made from. Native name-shapes survive here that were levelled everywhere else: the **-wyn** ending (*Talwyn*), soft *l* and *w*. Outsiders call it Mistspeech; Blood Elves call it the Standard and consider the accusation of drift to run the other way. *(Analogue: Welsh.)*

### Dock Cant — the contact language

[Raxmar](../moko/aethos/raxmar.md) is a port where every Ancestor group is represented on the docks, and its speech shows it. Inflection is gone, word order carries everything, and vocabulary is borrowed from wherever the last ship came from. *th* goes to /f/. Heavy on epithets and by-names in place of formal ones — **the Shark of Raxmar** is a Cant construction, and on the docks it is more use than *Umbro Augusto* would be. *(Analogue: a trade pidgin gone native.)*

### Void

No living dialect. [Void](../moko/void/void.md) kept its Founder place-names and lost every speaker who could parse them, which is exactly why "Void" survives as a name with nobody left who remembers it was a description.

### Off-world

The teleporters died with the Founders and each world's Standard has run isolated for three thousand years.

- **[Heaven](../solar-system/cinder/Heaven.md)** preserved the Founders' rank-and-command register with obsessive fidelity — a legion maintaining a chain of command maintains the language that encodes it. Heaven's Standard is the closest thing left to what the Founders actually sounded like, fossilized in ranks, codes, and titles.
- **[Hell](../solar-system/cinder/Hell.md)** discarded it. Where there's no law there's no need for the language of law, and Hell's speech has stripped down to what's needed to threaten, bargain, and lie.
- **[Fae](../solar-system/Fae.md)** warps it. The vocabulary is recognizably Standard, the grammar doesn't hold still.

## Religious Naming

Each faith has its own device, and they're already distinct in the corpus — worth keeping deliberate.

- **Church of the Founders** ([Vael](../moko/aethos/vael.md)) — Founder Standard, preserved. Names things by their function the way the Founders did: the **Chosen of Aethos** is a job description.
- **Church of the Missing** — names by absence and negation. *The Missing*, the vanished, the awaited. They venerate a gap.
- **Elliptism** — mirrored and reversible names, because the faith holds that creation and decay lead into each other. **Leon** and **Noel** are the same name run both directions, and circles are its symbol. Any new Elliptist name should be a palindrome, a reversal, or a matched pair.

## Naming by Category

| Category | Layer | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Worlds | Founder | Short, blunt, unsuffixed. An assay verdict. *Moko, Null, Cinder, Fae* |
| Continents | Founder | Root + **-os/-ath/-oth**, or a bare function word. *Aethos, Dorath, Void* |
| Founder-era cities and installations | Founder | Root + **-ael/-mar**. *Vael, Raxmar* |
| Post-War settlements | Vernacular | Descriptor + Function compound. *Hexguard, Flergburg, Mysthaven* |
| Countries | Either, per founding era | *Vall* (native/Vallic), *Mysthaven* (vernacular exonym) |
| People, administrative class | Founder | Given **-us/-a** + lineage **-o** |
| People, common | Either | Single name, dialect-shaped |
| Flora and fauna | Vernacular | Always compounds. Never Latinate — ordinary people named these after the Founders died. |
| Founder technology and processes | Founder | Flat functional labels and euphemisms. *Magic Metal, Null Metal, Null Field, the Quota, the Silence* |
| Titles and epithets | Dock Cant | *the Shark of Raxmar* |

## Existing Names, Audited

Everything in the current files passes, including stress and phonology, with three cases worth an in-world explanation rather than a rename:

- **Orm** — a Norse-shaped name on a [Mysthaven](../moko/dorath/mysthaven.md) city, where Mistspeech shapes should apply. Reading: *Orm* is the **Vallic** name, from *orm*, "worm." It is the only Mysthaven city the Vallerains ever found, and the invaders' name for it is the one that got written down and burned into the record. Its Blood Elf endonym is not widely known outside Mysthaven.

- **Mysthaven** — a transparent vernacular compound naming a country whose people speak the most conservative dialect on the planet. Reading: it is an **exonym**. Blood Elf cities are "very hard to find unless you know where you are going," so the name outsiders use for the place is an outsider's description of it — mist, and a haven. The endonym is a Mistspeech form and would be built to those rules.

- **Vallerain** — a Latinate *-ain* demonym on a Vallic country name. Reading: this is the **Vaelish** demonym, coined by Aethos administrators who needed a word for those people and built it out of their own dialect. Vallic speakers call themselves something else at home. Demonyms crossing register like this is ordinary — outsiders name peoples more often than peoples name themselves.

---

**When coining anything new, ask one question first: who named this, and when?** Founders and their inheritors get Layer 1. Everyone since gets Layer 2. That single decision determines the sounds, the shape, the suffix, and the register, and it is why the names will stop feeling random.
