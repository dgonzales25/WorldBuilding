# Magic

## Overview

Magic in this world is energy. There is no creating material from nothing, but there is the ability to enhance something that is already there — you can't just make fire appear, there needs to be a source. However, this can be used to incorporate different elements or make attacks stronger or weaker. Putting magical energy into fire will make it burn hotter and bigger. It takes magical stamina to do things for a long time or learn how to channel energy quickly — levitating a small object is easy, but levitating a city is impossible.

Magic can be flared to push magical energy out without a focus. While in air, this causes magic dust to be created, which will disappear.

Magical energy can be trapped inside the lattice structures of materials. Eventually magical energy decays, but at a slow rate. Magical energy radiates from charged creatures and items at a rate based on the charge, causing a slow, gradual decay in magical power. It is possible to measure this radiation to gauge someone's magical reserves — the more stored, the more that radiates off.

[Magic Metal](Magic%20Metal.md) combines a lattice to trap magic with a mechanism to place magical energy into the lattice. It refills over time, but takes time. It can act as a superconductor since it can absorb magical energy at the rate it loses it from transporting it.

## Source

The source of magical energy is the core of the planet. Magical energy radiates out of the planet into space.

Lava has a higher magical charge due to it coming from deeper in the planet.

## Conductors

Carbon is a conductor of magic, which allows most organic structures to conduct magic. Magic can conduct through skin.

## Processing

Most creatures have a process where empty lattice cells move to the walls of their veins. As magical energy passes through a cell it gets charged and detaches, then starts flowing in the blood. When a creature uses magical energy, these cells move to the walls of the veins to pass the energy to the creature. There is a limit to how much magical energy can be pulled or stored in the blood, though this can be increased with training.

Using magic takes effort like flexing a muscle, since you're extracting the magical energy from your blood — you can only do it for so long, and the more you do it the easier it becomes to cast bigger spells. Creatures may or may not continuously use magic; a warrior might swing harder by drawing on magical power without ever realizing they're using magic.

## Repulsiveness

Two sources of magical energy repel each other — like charges repel. [Null Metal](Null%20Metal.md) carries the opposite charge, so it's the one exception: instead of being pushed away by magic, it's pulled toward it.

On its own this force is faint, on the order of a pound, whether it's the repulsion between two charged objects or the pull toward something null. It's rarely strong enough to notice in the moment — but it never stops, and applied for centuries or millennia even a pound of constant push or pull is enough to keep dust from settling on an exposed surface, or to work a weak spot loose.

Flaring changes the scale entirely: a creature can push far more magical energy through themselves for a brief moment, spiking that same force by orders of magnitude — enough to physically deflect an incoming weapon. This takes skill and can't be sustained; it's a controlled overload of the ambient baseline, not a different force.

## Magic items

Enchanted magical items radiate magical energy and slowly lose their magic properties unless recharged by something or someone.

Potions are also magical items that slowly lose their magic potency over time.

Items made from [Magic Metal](Magic%20Metal.md) will not lose their magical ability.

## Power level

Magic is basically like a muscle and needs to be exercised to grow in power. However, as creatures get older they don't lose magical ability.
