# Magic Metal

## What is it
Magic metal is a magically infused iron. The source is blood of an creature. Blood is one of the few ways magic can bind to something. Magic from a source is absorbed and processed by the body and stored in blood. Iron from the blood can be separated. 

Magic metal is the purest form of condensed magical energy. To create magic metal you must compress organic magical material (blood) under immense pressure and heat (similar to compressing carbon into diamond). 

## Properties 

Magic metal has many properties that can change depending on the way it was created. These can also be refine for any given piece of magic metal.

### Superconductivity 
It can be made into is a superconductor for magical energy and electrical energy. This is the reason the [Founders](../species/Founders.md) harvested the creatures of this world. 

### Absorption 
Due to its superconductivity magic metal can absorb energy faster than it radiates it back out. Pumping in a steady stream of magical current — from a battery, a reactor, anything actively feeding it — raises its effective charge far past what it holds at rest, without burning out the lattice the way that current would burn out an ordinary conductor.

### Hardness/Strength 
Magic metal is extremely hard but not impossible to scratch. Stronger than all other metals





## Forging
Magic metal can be combined with other non magical metals to dilute it but can not be combined with metals that have a different magic metal source. This means that large chunks of magic metal are extremely valuable since the size of pure magic metal can not be increased.

It takes the blood of about 400 people to make enough metal for a sword.

## History
Magic metal was invented by [The Founders](../species/Founders.md) in their quest for eternal life. It can be found naturally in fossils and in the ground but it is extremely rare, since it requires a magical being to die in a way that they don't decompose.

## Canyons

These trenches were dug for the Founders' levitating rail network, not the teleporters — ordinary excavation, no magic involved in the digging itself, just a straight bed to bury a Magic Metal conduit in. The conduit itself never had to be anything more than an inert rail: buried in solid rock, it had the whole mass of the ground to brace against, so the reaction force had somewhere to go and the track just sat there instead of getting shoved around by its own repulsion. The trains carried the active half — a Magic Metal skid on the underside, fed by an onboard reactor drawing off batteries the same way the [Founders'](../species/Founders.md) teleporters did, pumping a continuous charge into the skid far past anything the metal would hold on its own. [Superconductivity](#superconductivity) is what made that survivable: an ordinary conductor pushed that hard would burn out, but the skid could carry a charge in the same range a creature reaches by flaring — except sustained for as long as the reactor kept feeding it, which no living creature's own reserves could do. Repelled off the buried conduit at that strength, a train would hang a few feet above the trench floor for the length of the journey.

The conduit's resting charge hasn't changed since the day it was buried — it was never anything but passive, before the Virus War or after. What's kept the trenches from healing over the way any abandoned ditch eventually would is that same faint, passive repulsion (see [Magic — Repulsiveness](Magic.md#repulsiveness)): nowhere near strong enough to lift so much as a leaf, but it doesn't need to be — it only has to keep windblown dust from settling and scree from cementing along the walls, the same slow, constant push that builds [Null Metal](Null%20Metal.md#ridges) deposits into mounds instead of leaving them bare. What's gone is the trains and the reactors that rode them — nothing has run this network for thousands of years since the [Virus War](../species/Founders.md#virus-war) — so all that's left is dead-straight canyons with a dark, unweathered seam of conduit exposed along the floor.

Dead straight is a relative term, though. Magic Metal is the hardest material there is, but hardness isn't immunity to the ground itself moving — over thousands of years, plate tectonics has sheared a handful of these conduits clean through at fault lines, offsetting the canyon on either side by anywhere from a few feet to a few hundred yards. Anyone patient enough to survey a break and match the offset back up would find the two halves line up dead straight again, which is a much harder thing to explain away as coincidence than the canyon itself.

Centuries of people digging into these canyons for a share of the metal means the accessible stretches have mostly been picked clean by now — chipped, pried, and hauled off wherever the seam ran shallow enough to reach with hand tools. What's left tends to be in the harder-to-reach spots: deep pockets below where earlier generations gave up, remote stretches nobody's bothered marching tools out to, or a freshly exposed cross-section at a break nobody's found yet. A mined-out stretch also stops holding itself together — with the source charge gone, dust and scree start settling into it again like any ordinary ditch — so a canyon's floor tells its own history: bare, dark, and pristine where the seam is still intact, slowly filling back in wherever it's already been stripped.
