# Overview

Orcs are [Ancestors](Ancestors.md), bred as the enforcement arm of the Founders' labor system on [Aethos](../../moko/aethos/aethos.md), the farming continent where most of the labor force actually lived and worked. When a quota wasn't met, an Orc overseer was the one who marked the name, put the story of "reassignment" together, and walked the condemned to the transport that only ever went one way — see [Void — The Quota](../../moko/void/void.md#the-quota). They never knew where the transports really went either; the silence went all the way up past them too.

Like [Goliaths](Goliaths.md), Orcs never settled into one city of their own. After the Virus War, their size and strength found new purpose breaking ground for Aethos's farms — clearing forest, moving stone, terracing slopes, raising barns and silos — work that's needed everywhere Wood Elves and Humans farm, so Orc crews are a fixture in nearly every Aethos settlement rather than concentrated in one place.

Orcs also tend to run hot — quick to anger and slow to let a grudge go cold, whatever the reason for it. That's a temperament, not a trade; it has nothing to do with the work most Orcs actually spend their days on.

## Society Flaws

Being the visible hand of the Quota — not its architects — left Orc society with nowhere to put the blame but on itself. Older generations raised their children to treat hardness as a virtue, teaching them to stop asking what happened to the people marked for "reassignment" long before they were old enough to understand what the silence was protecting them from. That inherited suppression didn't end with the Founders, and neither did the reputation: other Ancestor groups still read Orcs as enforcers first and builders second, no matter how many generations have passed since an Orc actually ran a quota. The temper doesn't help — a rare shouting match at a job site is the story that spreads, not the years of careful work that came before it — so most Orc crews spend as much time correcting the assumption as they do the actual building.
